# 2035 NFL Legacy Floor ($S_{\text{all-time}}$) Projections

To recalculate the **Legacy Floor ($S_{\text{all-time}}$)** for the year 2035, the model accounts for the nine seasons of simulated outcomes (2026–2034) while incorporating the current generation of active players who will be eligible for the Pro Football Hall of Fame by that time.

### 1. Championship Equity ($S_{\text{Championship}}$) Adjustments
Over the 9-season simulation window, hardware was distributed as follows[cite: 2]:
* **Baltimore Ravens (+20 CP):** 2 Super Bowl Wins (2030, 2033)[cite: 2].
* **Tennessee Titans (+13 CP):** 1 Super Bowl Win (2031), 1 Runner-Up (2026)[cite: 2].
* **New York Giants (+12 CP):** 4 Super Bowl Runner-Ups (2027, 2030, 2031, 2033)[cite: 2].
* **1 Super Bowl Win (+10 CP):** Rams (2026), Broncos (2027), Eagles (2028), Lions (2029), Cardinals (2032), Panthers (2034)[cite: 2].
* **Miami Dolphins (+6 CP):** 2 Super Bowl Runner-Ups (2028, 2032)[cite: 2].
* **1 Super Bowl Runner-Up (+3 CP):** Bengals (2029), Jets (2034)[cite: 2].

Because no team surpassed the historical ceiling of the New England Patriots ($75.0$ raw CP)[cite: 1], the normalization denominator ($CP_{\max}$) remains unchanged[cite: 1]. 

### 2. Canton Alumni Share ($S_{\text{Canton}}$) Adjustments
Projecting into 2035 means factoring in a 9-season increase to the franchise age denominator ($\text{Seasons}_i$)[cite: 1], which slightly decays the $\text{HOF}/\text{Seasons}$ efficiency rate for all organizations[cite: 1]. To approximate this for the formula, the baseline $S_{\text{Canton}}$ undergoes a $\approx 10\%$ rate dilution due to the 9-season age increase, while each new generational HOF lock (e.g., Patrick Mahomes and Travis Kelce for Kansas City; Lamar Jackson and Justin Tucker for Baltimore; T.J. Watt for Pittsburgh; Trent Williams for San Francisco) adds roughly $+3.8$ points back to the organization's Canton index.
